How it works

The request becomes a numeric contract

Language and level generation are kept apart: the language model only writes eight numbers, and a language-free policy builds and checks the level. That is why every miss can be traced to the translation, the generator, or conflicting targets.

A · Language bridge

Eight numbers

GPT-5.2 maps the request to targets in [0, 1] for enemies, coins, blocks, pipes, gaps, verticality, smoothness and platforms. It never places a tile.

B · Stitch

Pieces of real levels

A MaskablePPO policy stitches about ten of 264 pieces cut from the 15 original Super Mario Bros. levels (converted by the VGLC), joined only where Mario can jump across.

C · Safe edit

Fix what the pieces can't

The same policy applies edits (remove enemies, fill gaps, flatten terrain…). Any edit that breaks the level is undone.

D · Touch-up & check

Verified delivery

A simple rule-based touch-up fixes what is left, and the A* game-playing bot must finish the level before it is released.

What is real here

  • Every level was generated by the released 10M-step policy (best of up to 64 runs, with or without the touch-up, whichever is closer to the request) and completed by the Mario AI Framework A* agent.
  • Only levels that match their request are shown: mean error at most 0.05, no number off by more than 0.15, and “none” really means none.
  • Every “In words” request uses a translation recorded from GPT-5.2, replayed exactly as in the paper.
  • The animation replays the real episode, decision by decision.

What this page simulates

  • No model runs in your browser: free text is matched to the closest recorded request.
  • “In numbers” shows the generated level whose target is closest to your sliders.
  • The playable game uses simplified physics, not the original engine.
